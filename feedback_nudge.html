Three-study research program

From Reminders to Independent Action

How can Canvas or Moodle guidance help students act now without making later action depend on continued experimental reminders?

How the framework grows

Study 1 tests whether explaining the feedback–action connection improves meaningful initiation. Study 2 adds cue design and randomized support withdrawal. Study 3 adds the student's role in constructing the plan.

Primary behavior: saving a task-relevant first response at least 48 hours before the assignment deadline—not simply opening the assignment.

Study 2 — Test what survives when support stops

Is withdrawal less disruptive when the plan refers to a recurring course event rather than an experimental reminder?
Retained from Study 1
Linked feedback and plan ⇢ Understanding and relevance ⇢ Meaningful initiation

Linked guidance is held constant while cue design changes.

New randomized factors

Cue: experimental reminder versus recurring course event.

Follow-up support: continued versus withdrawn.

⇢

Proposed explanation

The recurring event remains available after reminders stop. Action may continue to follow that event.

Cue-aligned behavior is supporting evidence, not proof of automatic habit.

⇢

New follow-up outcome

Timely meaningful initiation under continued versus withdrawn support.

Also assess completion and work quality.

Direct randomized test: cue type × withdrawal → follow-up behavior.

Prediction: the withdrawal penalty is smaller under the independent-cue design.

Four follow-up groups

Study 2: 2 × 2 design
Cue type Continued support Withdrawn support
Experimental reminder S2-1: reminder cue + continued messages S2-2: reminder cue + no new messages
Recurring course event S2-3: class-end cue + continued messages S2-4: class-end cue + no new messages
Withdrawal penalty = continued-support rate − withdrawn-support rate

Predicted contrast: penalty for reminder cue > penalty for class-end cue
Interpretation check: A small penalty is not enough if behavior is poor in both support conditions. Report absolute performance as well as the interaction.

The estimand is a cue-design policy effect. Delivery, reading time, actual class end, and student availability cannot be made perfectly identical.

Four workflows, each crossed with support

Study 3: 4 × 2 design
Workflow Student participation Continue Withdraw
T1. Rule-based plan Accept or decline a complete plan T1-C T1-W
T2. AI draft, no editing Accept or decline a read-only draft T2-C T2-W
T3. Editable AI draft Review, optionally revise, then adopt or decline T3-C T3-W
T4. Self-written plan Create a plan using a structured template T4-C T4-W

Held comparable: behavioral target, feedback, available information, permissible independent cues, feasible actions, assignment access, and the right to decline.

T2 versus T3: use the same initial AI drafting procedure. Editing is available in T3 but is never compulsory.

Do not label this a pure ownership effect. Editing can change fit, comprehension, effort, and final plan content. Measure these separately. No universal ranking of the four workflows is assumed.

The central distinction

Improving behavior while support is present is not the same as sustaining behavior when proactive support is removed.

  • Study 1: Does the connection help?
  • Study 2: Does the trigger survive?
  • Study 3: Does participation help, and at what cost?

“Independent action” means action without new experimental prompts. It does not mean action without normal course resources, saved plans, calendars, or accessibility support. Four weeks of follow-up does not establish permanent change or automatic habit.
